EXTERNAL REVIEW OF report/REPORT.md (+ figures/captions.md, figures/fig1-7)
Reviewer perspective: evolutionary genomics, statistics, and a reader from the broader scientific audience.
Basis: PROMPT.md (Reporting + WRITING STANDARDS), PREREG.md, REPORT.md, captions.md, fig1-7 PNGs, and spot checks of results/.
Date: 2026-10-29. The report was not edited.

OVERALL VERDICT
The design is careful, the main null result is honestly stated, and the method-validation material is the strongest part. The problems are
(a) four conclusions that go beyond the data shown: co-option "fits best", "the only convergent loss is the flagellum", the
1.7-2.2-fold stem-specific bias called "strong", and "every per-stem excess is within" that bias, which the data contradict;
(b) one headline number that is misleading: 81.9% of gains called "sequence-novel" is dominated by species-specific gains on terminal
branches, while Figure 4a shows 47%;
(c) the pre-registration timeline is described more cleanly than PREREG.md records it;
(d) jargon and internal labels a general reader cannot follow; and
(e) length. The prose is about 10,500 words excluding HTML comments and tables (about 10,280 without the human disclaimer). The target
is 8,000-9,000.

PART 1. THE TEN MOST IMPORTANT PROBLEMS (ranked)
======================================================================================================================

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The co-option conclusion is overstated. The index the report cites as support is equally high on control branches and is LOWER
   on transition stems than on controls in 4 of 7 transitions.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: results/exploratory/euk/summary.md §2. Median of the pfam_expanded index over matched unicellular controls: 0.977-0.990.
Percentile of each transition among its controls: animals 0.024, streptophytes 0.024, fungal hyphae 0.071, dictyostelids 0.119.
Mean percentile over the 7 transitions: 0.39. The HOG-based version points the other way (mean percentile 0.77). The "presence"
positive controls (cadherin, SH2-kinase, TALE, RB) were chosen from the co-option papers, so their passing validates the pipeline,
not the hypothesis. §5 itself says "some agreement is built in".

(a) Abstract
QUOTE: "In every lineage, 80–100% of the domain families expanded in the multicellular clade were already present in its unicellular
ancestor. This agrees with the co-option view, but the measure is descriptive: families shared with the sister lineage count as
ancestral by construction."
REPLACE WITH: "In every lineage, 80–100% of the protein-domain families that expanded in the multicellular clade were already present
in its unicellular ancestor. The same measure is 97–99% on comparable unicellular branches, so a high value is expected for any
branch and does not by itself favour co-option."

(b) §7 lead
QUOTE: "Read together with the positive controls, the results fit the co-option view best."
REPLACE WITH: "The results are compatible with the co-option view, but this design could not tell it apart from a modest burst of
novelty."

(c) §7.1
QUOTE: "Our data agree descriptively: 80–100% of expanded Pfam families were present in the unicellular ancestor of every
transition, and the presence controls passed (cadherin repeats, SH2–kinase pairs, TALE homeobox, RB pathway)."
REPLACE WITH: "Our data are compatible with this but do not discriminate. 80–100% of expanded Pfam families were present in the
unicellular ancestor of every transition, against 97–99% on matched unicellular control branches. Four of the seven transitions rank
below most of their controls (percentiles 0.02–0.12). The presence controls (cadherin repeats, SH2–kinase pairs, TALE homeobox, RB
pathway) show that the pipeline recovers families the co-option literature documents. Because they were chosen from that
literature, they validate the method, not the hypothesis."

(d) §8, third bullet
QUOTE: "*Strength: descriptive agreement with the co-option literature; the comparative part is weak.*"
REPLACE WITH: "*Strength: weak. The index is equally high on unicellular control branches (97–99%), so it does not distinguish
co-option from novelty.*"
In the same bullet, also change "Most families expanded in multicellular clades predate the transition" to "Most families expanded
in multicellular clades predate the transition, as they do on any branch".

2. The stem-specific gain bias ("1.7–2.2-fold") is reported as firm, and §7.1 makes a claim about it that the data contradict.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: results/calibration_tree/summary.md.
- The estimate comes from ONE simulation draw per setting: 7 stems vs 9 control branches, with a simulator that has no gene birth.
  The Poisson intervals ignore over-dispersion.
- On the scale of the actual test statistic, the shift for sequence-novel gains is +0.064 and −0.036, which the file itself calls
  "not distinguishable from 0".
- The ratio arises mainly because CONTROL branches carry fewer spurious gains than predicted (O/E 0.5–0.6). Stems are about as
  predicted (O/E 0.9–1.2).
- Per-stem ratios to the regression prediction (per_transition_H1H2.tsv, exp(resid_s)): animals 2.40-fold, volvocines 6.1-fold
  (88 vs 14). Both are OUTSIDE 1.7–2.2.

(a) Abstract
QUOTE: "and inferred gene gains were inflated, more so on transition branches (1.7–2.2-fold) than on controls."
(As written, this reads as "stems are inflated 1.7–2.2-fold".)
REPLACE WITH: "and inferred gene gains were inflated 2.1–2.6-fold on simulated genomes. One simulation on our own tree suggested that
transition branches may carry about twice as many spurious gains as control branches."

(b) §6.1, "Simulations and tree calibration"
QUOTE: "After adjusting for branch length and clade size, stems carried 1.7–2.2 times more spurious gains than control branches,
while losses and duplications did not differ (shift of the test statistic +0.02 to +0.09, within noise)."
REPLACE WITH: "After adjusting for branch length and clade size, stems carried 1.7–2.2 times as many spurious gains as control
branches. This is mostly because control branches carried fewer spurious gains than other internal branches (0.5–0.6 of the
expected number), not because stems carried more (0.9–1.2). The estimate rests on one simulation draw per setting, 7 stems and 9
control branches, and a simulator without true gene birth. On the scale of the test statistic itself, the shift for sequence-novel
gains was +0.06 and −0.04 in the two settings, within noise. Losses and duplications did not differ."

(c) §7.1
QUOTE: "Every per-stem excess is within the 1.7–2.2-fold extra inflation that our calibration attributes to the method on stems."
REPLACE WITH: "Two per-stem excesses exceed the calibrated bias range: animals (2.4-fold over the regression prediction) and
volvocines (about 6-fold, but from a predicted count of only 14). Method bias alone therefore may not explain every stem, although
the test across all seven stems was not significant."

(d) §8, sixth bullet
QUOTE: "gains are inflated more on stems (1.7–2.2-fold), and 52% of pre-animal duplication products are animal-only. *Strength:
strong; shown by simulation, calibration and diagnosed control failures.*"
REPLACE WITH: "gains are inflated 2.1–2.6-fold in simulations, possibly more on stems than on controls, and 52% of pre-animal
duplication products are animal-only. *Strength: strong for gain inflation and duplication misplacement; preliminary (one
simulation draw) for the stem-specific excess.*"

3. The headline gain-ladder percentage is misleading and contradicts Figure 4a.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: results/gainladder/euk/branch_gain_ladder.tsv.
- 87,524 of the 122,945 HOG gains (71%) lie on 22 TERMINAL (leaf) branches. These are species-specific groups, and 96% of them are
  "novel" almost trivially.
- On the 85 internal branches, 16,709 of 35,421 gains are sequence-novel (47%). This is what Figure 4a shows.
- The summary file mislabels the 122,945 total as "Ladder totals (internal branches)". Fix the source too.
- The text then compares only stems (84%) with unicellular-clade branches (66%). It leaves out mixed clades (40%) and root children
  (32%), which makes stems look unusual.

(a) §6.1
QUOTE: "Of the 122,945 HOG gains on the 107 branches searched, the DIAMOND novelty search classified 81.9% as *sequence-novel*: no
representative protein had a hit outside the clade. Another 17.1% had an outside relative, 1.3% had outside relatives only in the
other domain of life (candidates for horizontal gene transfer), and 1.0% could be re-joined to a family lost on the same branch."
REPLACE WITH: "On the 85 internal branches searched, 47% of 35,421 HOG gains were *sequence-novel*: no representative protein had a
detectable relative outside the clade (Figure 4a). The other 22 searched branches lead to single species. There, most gains are
species-specific groups that are almost all 'novel', which raises the overall figure to 82% of 122,945. About 1% of gains had
relatives only in the other domain of life (candidates for horizontal gene transfer)."

(b) §6.1
QUOTE: "The share called sequence-novel was 83.6% on transition stems and 65.5% on branches inside unicellular lineages."
REPLACE WITH: "The share called sequence-novel was 84% on transition stems, 66% inside unicellular lineages, 40% on branches whose
descendants include both unicellular and multicellular species, and 32% on the two branches next to the root."

4. "The only convergent loss was the flagellum" goes beyond what was shown.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: results/robustness/exploratory/summary.md §1 and Fig 7c.
- Cilium/flagellum domains are only 15% of the 421 convergently lost domains.
- With the ORIGINAL null, removing the 358 cilium-linked domains still leaves 258 vs 170 expected (p = 0.17; Fig 7c shows this, the
  text omits it). The excess disappears only under a second, post-hoc null that preserves each lineage's loss count.
- The excess is mostly the fungal-hyphae × dictyostelid pair (177 vs 61), and loss counts fall with the number of sampled
  multicellular species (ρ = −0.58). This is a data-structure effect, not shown to be flagellar.
- The primary flagellum test only examined flagellar genes, so it cannot show that nothing else was lost convergently.

(a) Abstract
QUOTE: "The only convergent loss was the flagellum, in fungi and dictyostelids."
REPLACE WITH: "The clearest convergent loss was the flagellum, in fungi and dictyostelids, where it was expected. A broader excess of
convergently lost domains did not survive removal of dictyostelids or of transcriptome-based proteomes."

(b) §6.4 paragraph heading
QUOTE: "**Convergent domain loss: disappears, and turns out to be the flagellum.**"
REPLACE WITH: "**Convergent domain loss: driven by one pair of lineages and by data quality; flagellar domains are enriched.**"

(c) §6.4, same paragraph
QUOTE: "Excluding them leaves no signal (258 vs 264 under a null that preserves each transition's number of losses; p = 0.70).
<!-- ... --> The only convergent loss is the H12 flagellum loss seen again."
REPLACE WITH: "Excluding them leaves a smaller excess (258 vs 170 expected; p = 0.17). That excess disappears under a null that
preserves each transition's total number of losses (258 vs 264; p = 0.70), but this null was chosen after the results were seen.
Flagellar domains therefore explain part of the excess. The rest tracks the dictyostelids and the number of species sampled per
clade (fewer losses are called when more species are sampled; ρ = −0.58)."

(d) §7.1
QUOTE: "The only convergent loss in our data is the flagellum, in fungi and dictyostelids, where it was expected. No other function
is lost convergently once flagellar domains are removed (p = 0.70)."
REPLACE WITH: "The clearest convergent loss in our data is the flagellum, in fungi and dictyostelids, where it was expected. No excess
of convergent loss remains once flagellar domains are removed and the null keeps each lineage's total number of losses (p = 0.70;
a post-hoc null)."

(e) §8, fourth bullet
QUOTE: "**The only convergent loss is the flagellum**"
REPLACE WITH: "**The one clear convergent loss is the flagellum**"

6. The rate test (H9) is framed as more decisive than it is, and its controls are mis-described.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence: results/h9/summary.md and h9_tests.tsv.
- The pooled interval treats the 7 stem-minus-control-mean differences as independent, although all 7 share the SAME 10 controls.
  The uncertainty of the control mean does not average out over the 7 stems, so the 0.96–1.26 interval is too narrow. My rough
  recomputation is about 0.92–1.31, which still excludes 1.5. Recompute properly.
- "Unicellular control pairs" is wrong: in 9 of 10 controls one descendant clade contains multicellular species (§5 says this).
- Sensitivity S3 (unicellular tips only) gives a pooled rate ratio of 1.16 [1.01, 1.34], which EXCLUDES 1. The report calls this
  "agree".
- The p-value ranges 0.19–0.61 across the five pre-registered orientation seeds.
- The measure covers all evolution after P, not only the stem.
- The pooled ratio is an AVERAGE: per-transition ratios run from 0.81 to 1.36.

(a) Abstract
QUOTE: "The rate test was sensitive: it excluded a 1.5-fold genome-wide rate shift (pooled ratio 1.10, 95% CI 0.96–1.26), and we
found no within-protein sign of relaxed selection."
REPLACE WITH: "The rate test had the most statistical power. Averaged over the seven transitions, conserved proteins did not evolve
1.5-fold faster or slower on the multicellular side (pooled rate ratio 1.10, 95% CI 0.96–1.26), although individual transitions
ranged from 0.81 to 1.36. A within-protein measure of relaxed constraint showed no consistent shift."

(b) §6.3
QUOTE: "Yet the 10 unicellular control pairs, randomly oriented, already span log ratios from −0.26 to +0.36"
REPLACE WITH: "Yet the 10 control pairs, randomly oriented, already span log ratios from −0.26 to +0.36. Each pair splits at a
unicellular ancestor, but in 9 of the 10 one side includes multicellular species."

(c) §6.3
QUOTE: "Two sensitivities agree: using genome-derived species only gives p = 0.63, and using only unicellular tips in the controls
gives p = 0.146 (rate ratio 1.16 [1.01, 1.34])."
REPLACE WITH: "Two sensitivity analyses give the same test result: p = 0.63 with genome-derived species only, and p = 0.146 with only
unicellular tips in the controls. In the second, however, the pooled rate ratio (1.16, 1.01–1.34) excludes 1. This is a weak hint of
faster evolution on the multicellular side, still well below 1.5-fold."

(d) §6.3, add after "which excludes a 1.5-fold shift"
ADD: "This interval treats the seven comparisons as independent, although they share the same ten controls, so it is somewhat too
narrow."

7. The gene-content tests: power is understated in the abstract, the H1/H2 effect size is not the tested quantity, a robustness check
   uses the null the report rejected, and the H4/H5 measurement problem is missing from the abstract.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Evidence:
- The power table gives 2.4–5.9-fold for H1/H2 and odds ratios 2.2–2.8 for H4/H5. The fitted over-dispersion (0.77) is close to the
  pessimistic (5.9-fold) scenario.
- The Table 2 pooled effect for H1/H2 (1.67) is observed vs the RAW mean of the matched controls (per_transition_H1H2.tsv, column
  est). The test uses the regression residual. For animals the first is 4.6-fold, the second 2.4-fold. A reader who sees "1.67
  pooled" next to "animals 594 vs 247" cannot reconcile them.
- The leave-one-out p-values (loo.tsv) use the PERMUTATION scheme. §4.3 calls that scheme anti-conservative for this test, and its
  whole-data p is 0.084, not 0.113.
- H4/H5 count stem duplications, the quantity whose placement failed control C07 and moves for 38% of duplications on real control
  branches.

(a) Abstract
QUOTE: "The gene-content tests could detect only effects of about 2-fold or more."
REPLACE WITH: "The gene-content tests could detect only large effects. They needed roughly a 2.2–2.8-fold change in the share of
regulatory or adhesion genes among duplications, and a 2.4- to 6-fold excess of new genes, with our data near the less favourable
end. The two duplication tests also depend on duplication timing, which the positive controls showed to be unreliable; orthology-free
versions gave the same result."

(b) §6.2, add after Table 2
ADD: "For the gain test, the pooled effect compares observed gains with the average of the matched controls. The test itself, and
the per-stem ratios below (e.g. animals 2.4-fold), compare observed gains with a regression prediction that adjusts for branch
length and clade size. The two quantities differ."

(c) §6.2
QUOTE: "Leaving out any one transition gives p = 0.052–0.181."
REPLACE WITH: "Under the permutation scheme (whole-data p = 0.084), leaving out any one transition gives p = 0.052–0.181. This scheme
is anti-conservative for this test (Section 4.3), so the true values are higher."

(d) §7.2
QUOTE: "(about 2.4-fold or more for novel gains, odds ratios of 2.2–2.8 for toolkit shares)"
REPLACE WITH: "(about 2.4-fold or more for novel gains under moderate over-dispersion, closer to 6-fold at the over-dispersion we
observed; odds ratios of 2.2–2.8 for toolkit shares)"

(e) §8, first bullet
QUOTE: "*Strength: moderate for effects of ≥2.4-fold; uninformative for smaller ones (power 13–35% at 1.5-fold).*"
REPLACE WITH: "*Strength: moderate for effects of roughly 3- to 6-fold; uninformative for smaller ones (power 13–35% at 1.5-fold).*"
