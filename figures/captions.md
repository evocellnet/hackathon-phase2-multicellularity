# Figure captions

**Figure 1. The eukaryote guide tree, its 187 proteomes and the seven primary transitions.** Branch order and grouping only; branch lengths are not drawn. Tip symbols give each species' state: unicellular (grey circle), simple multicellular (blue circle), complex multicellular (blue square), grey zone such as colonial, coenocytic, plasmodial or facultative forms (orange diamond), and reversal to unicellularity (green triangle). An asterisk marks the 36 proteomes derived from transcriptomes. Thick black segments are the stems (P → M) of the seven pre-registered primary transitions, numbered as in the key and in Table 1. Stems 2 (fungal hyphae) and 7 (volvocine colonies) are two-branch path stems. Light shading marks each multicellular crown clade M. Outer arcs label major clades. Sources: tree data/species_tree_bl_euk.nwk (topology identical to data/guide_tree_euk.nwk); states data/species_traits.tsv; stems data/transitions.tsv. Script: scripts/report/fig_trees.py.

**Figure 2. Workflow.** (1) Proteomes, guide trees and the transitions table. These inputs were frozen and hashed, together with the design, test specifications and power table, before any eukaryote output existed. (2) Ancestra reconstructs hierarchical orthologous groups (HOGs). A parser turns them into per-branch gains, losses and duplications. Gains are ranked on the gain-evidence ladder, because raw HOG gains were inflated 2.1–2.6-fold on simulations. Pfam domains and disorder annotate proteins, and 250 BUSCO marker genes give branch lengths and gene trees. (3) Seven primary stems are compared with a pool of 30 unicellular control branches in four gene-content tests (H1/H2, H4, H5, H12) and one rate test (H9), with Holm correction across the five tests. Analyses in the dashed box are exploratory. Script: scripts/report/fig_workflow.py.

**Figure S1. The prokaryote guide tree (82 genome-based proteomes).** Symbols as in Figure 1 (there are no reversals or transcriptomes). The four numbered stems are the secondary prokaryotic transitions. These are analysed descriptively only, because the tree yields just 2 eligible control branches. Sources: data/species_tree_bl_prok.nwk; data/species_traits.tsv; data/transitions.tsv. Script: scripts/report/fig_trees.py.

**Figure 3. Primary tests: no stem class stands out from its matched controls once the five tests are corrected for multiplicity.** Each panel is one pre-registered test. Each row is one of the seven primary transitions. Grey dots are the values of that transition's matched control branches: the 20 nearest of the 30-branch unicellular control pool, or for H9 the 10 pool branches with two child clades of at least 2 tips each, oriented at random. The blue diamond is the stem value. u is the stem's mid-rank percentile among its own controls (0.5 = typical, 1 = above all). T is the mean u over the n transitions. p comes from a permutation of stem and control labels (10,000 draws; one-sided for H4, H5 and H12, two-sided for H9) or, for H1/H2, from a parametric bootstrap (9,999 replicates, two-sided). Holm p is adjusted across the five primary tests. H1/H2: residual of sequence-novel gains (gains with no DIAMOND hit outside the clade) from a negative-binomial generalised linear model (NB-GLM) fitted on the control pool, with log branch length, log number of descendants, log number of sister tips and the transcriptome fraction as covariates. H4 and H5: logit of the share of stem-duplicated hierarchical orthologous groups (HOGs) that fall in the class, minus the logit of the class share at the parent node. The classes are transcription-factor DNA-binding domains (TF-DBD; H4) and adhesion/extracellular-matrix (ECM) or signalling domains (H5). Branches flagged for whole-genome duplication (WGD) are excluded. H12: f is the mean over 4 flagellar modules of the fraction of module HOGs present at the parent that are lost on the stem. It is signed by the frozen expected direction w (+1 loss expected, −1 not), so Red algae (no flagellum at P) are excluded and n = 6. H9: r is the median over BUSCO marker genes of the log ratio of mean path length from P to M tips versus P to sister tips. The H12 p (0.020, one-sided) is the only one below 0.05 before correction; its Holm p is 0.102. Sources: results/primary/euk/{holm.tsv, primary_tests.tsv, per_transition_*.tsv, branch_values.tsv, matched_sets.tsv}; results/h9/{h9_transitions.tsv, h9_controls.tsv, h9_tests.tsv}; H1/H2 control residuals from results/report/h1h2_branch_residuals.tsv (scripts/report/export_h1h2_residuals.py, which re-derives them with the analysis code and checks them against the committed stem values and u). Script: scripts/report/fig_results.py.

**Figure 4. Gain counts are the least certain quantity in the reconstruction.** (a) Gain-evidence ladder summed by branch class over the 85 internal branches of results/gainladder/euk/branch_gain_ladder.tsv. Orange: HOG gains (level 1, a HOG whose origin is on the branch). Blue: sequence-novel gains (level 3, no DIAMOND hit outside the clade), with their share of HOG gains in brackets. Mixed clades contain both unicellular and multicellular descendants. (b) Each primary stem (path stems summed over their edges): HOG gains (orange), sequence-novel gains y (blue) and the NB-GLM expectation ŷ (black tick) from the negative-binomial model fitted on the 30 control branches, the quantity tested in H1/H2 (Figure 3). (c) Ratio of inferred to true events for Ancestra (preset 'fast') on simulated genomes. Dots: 3 simulations with known truth (12, 16 and 24 species; internal branches). Squares: the calibration simulation on our pruned eukaryote tree (60 taxa), stem edges and control branches, default and high-turnover settings. Gains are inflated 2.1–2.5-fold in the simulations, while losses (1.1–1.5-fold) and duplications (about 1-fold) are close to the truth. On the calibration tree no true gains fall on stem or control branches (the simulator has no de novo gene birth), so the gain ratio is undefined (263 inferred vs 0 true). Sources: results/gainladder/euk/branch_gain_ladder.tsv; results/primary/euk/per_transition_H1H2.tsv; results/validation_hogparser/metrics.tsv; results/calibration_tree/class_summary.tsv. **Figure 7. EXPLORATORY: exploratory results and what survived post-hoc skeptical checks.** None of these analyses was pre-registered, none is in the Holm family, and the checks were designed after the results were seen, so they can only weaken a claim. (a) New multi-domain Pfam architectures on each primary stem. The plotted value is u, the stem's mid-rank percentile among its 20 matched control branches, computed on the residual from a negative-binomial generalised linear model (NB-GLM) fitted on the control branches. Four variants are shown: all species (blue diamond; T = mean u = 0.80, permutation p = 0.005, n = 7); BUSCO completeness added as a covariate (p = 0.017); genome-based species only (p = 0.0004); and only species with BUSCO completeness ≥ 80% (p = 0.16, n = 6; Red algae not evaluable). Leaving out one transition at a time keeps every p ≤ 0.017. The completeness filter removes the signal (p = 0.34 when Animals are also left out), so this result is suggestive, not robust. (b) Co-option index C: the share of Pfam families expanded on the stem that were already present at the parent node P. C is shown for each transition (blue) against the median of its 20 matched controls (black tick). u is the percentile among those controls, from the Pfam-based index; u HOG is the same index computed from toolkit hierarchical orthologous groups (HOGs), where there is no effect. Orange: Animals and Streptophytes with the multicellular clade subsampled to 3, 4 or 5 species (median and central 95% of 100 draws), next to the median of size-matched controls (grey tick). Both stay below every control median, but the result rests on Pfam annotation, which over-represents animal and plant families. (c) Convergent Pfam-domain loss: the number of domains lost on at least 2 primary stems (observed, blue) against the permutation null mean (open circle), with the permutation p. The full-data excess (421 vs 192, p = 0.043) disappears without Dictyostelids, in genome-only or BUSCO ≥ 80% species sets, and once the 358 cilium-linked domains are removed and the null keeps each transition's number of losses. The only convergent loss is therefore the flagellum loss in fungal hyphae and Dictyostelids, the same signal as H12. (d) AlphaFold structures of sequence-novel gains: 60 sampled gains per stem and 60 from that stem's matched controls (420 each in total). Left: share with no usable model (no AlphaFold Database model, or mean predicted local distance difference test (pLDDT) < 70). Stems 85.0% vs controls 78.8%; Mantel–Haenszel odds ratio (OR) 1.51 (95% confidence interval (CI) 1.06–2.15), Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel (CMH) p = 0.025, heterogeneous across stems. Right: share structurally old among gains with a usable model, i.e. a Foldseek hit (E ≤ 10⁻³, TM-score ≥ 0.5) to a Swiss-Prot protein from outside the gaining clade. Stems 15/63 (23.8%) vs controls 19/89 (21.3%); OR 0.96 (0.45–2.07), CMH p = 0.92. Lines are Wilson 95% CIs; numbers give k/n for stems vs controls. Sources: results/exploratory/euk/architectures/per_transition.tsv; results/exploratory/euk/cooption/per_transition.tsv; results/robustness/exploratory/{arch_per_transition.tsv, arch_checks.tsv, coopt_subsample_summary.tsv, loss_convergence_checks.tsv, loss_convergence_nonciliary.tsv, summary.md}; results/structure/{per_stem_swissprot.tsv, cmh_swissprot.tsv}. Script: scripts/report/fig_exploratory.py.
