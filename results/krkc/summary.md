# Kr/Kc — radical vs conservative replacements (within-protein constraint proxy) — EXPLORATORY

Generated by `scripts/phylo/krkc_run.py` (module `mcell/krkc.py`, tests `tests/test_krkc.py`).

**Changes.** Fitch-parsimony ancestral states on the pruned guide tree of each of the 250 BUSCO markers (trimmed alignments; taxa with < 50 % coverage removed; gaps = missing). Polytomies use the generalised Fitch rule, and ties are resolved by a seeded random choice.

**Classes.** Radical/conservative replacement classes follow Zhang (2000): charge {RHK}/{DE}/neutral, and polarity.

**Statistic.** LR = log Kr/Kc(M side) − log Kr/Kc(S side), pooled over genes, with a 95 % CI from 2,000 gene bootstraps.
- **Scope.** The M side is the stem plus the crown; the S side is the sister stem plus its crown. LR > 0 means proportionally more radical replacements on the multicellular side, which is what relaxed constraint predicts.
- **Reference.** The reference distribution comes from the 10 H9 control nodes (same random orientation as H9).
- **Validation.** The tests check hand-countable toy alignments, a polytomy, and a planted simulation (LR ≈ 1.0 for 4× up-weighted radical targets on the M side, CI > 0; null |LR| ≤ 0.02, CI covers 0, across 6 seeds).

## Reading for the H9 wording rule

Of the 4 primary transitions with H9 r > 0 (T_fungi_hyphae, T_dictyostelia, T_rhodophyta, T_volvocine_tgv), 1 show LR > 0 with both CIs above 0 (T_fungi_hyphae). The across-transition relabelling tests are not significant. Oriented control LR ranges: charge -0.201 to 0.351, polarity -0.132 to 0.116; the supported transitions' LR (T_fungi_hyphae: 0.032/0.042, charge/polarity) lie inside them, so they do not stand out from unicellular controls. "Relaxed constraint" can be worded for a transition only where its H9 r > 0 and its LR > 0 both hold and stand out from the control range above. Otherwise H9 stays a "rate shift".

**Caveats.**
- **Divergence bias.** Parsimony undercounts multiple hits, and in real proteins conservative replacements saturate first, so a faster or longer side can show a higher radical fraction without any relaxation. The H9 controls share this bias, so compare with their range rather than with 0.
- **Site opportunities.** Opportunities are not normalised (the same alignment columns are used on both sides).
- **Polarity scheme.** G and C are counted as polar (Zhang 2000).
