# Calibration on our tree: Ancestra bias by branch class (DESIGN §3.2)

Generated by `scripts/calibration_tree.py` (seed 11). Tables: `class_summary.tsv`, `stem_bias.tsv`, `branch_comparison.tsv`, `kept_tips.tsv`.

* Tree: eukaryote guide topology pruned to 60 taxa keeping every primary stem's P/path/M structure (>= 2 M-side tips spanning M's split, >= 2 sister-side tips); branch lengths from `data/species_tree_bl_euk.nwk`, rescaled to mean root-to-tip 1.0.
* Simulator: `ancestra.simulate`, 2000 root genes, defaults (dup = loss = 0.2) and high turnover (dup = loss = 0.4); Ancestra `--preset fast`; gain ladder with very-sensitive DIAMOND (real-DB e-values).
* Internal branches by class on the pruned tree: {'other_internal': 33, 'control_U': 9, 'primary_stem_edge': 9, 'root_child': 2} (U recomputed by `build_branch_table` on the pruned tree; 7 primary stems, path stems summed over edges).
* Caveat: the simulator has no de-novo gene birth; true gains are families surviving only in a clade (lost elsewhere), so true per-branch gain counts are small and ratios are noisy.

## Interpretation

* Internal-branch true gains are ~0 in this simulator (no gene birth), so stem and U gains are spurious; the question is purely whether spurious gains differ between stems and controls.
* Losses and duplications: stems and U inflated alike (pooled ratios ~1.1-1.3 in both; T_bias +0.021, +0.086, +0.043, +0.086).
* Gains: on the u scale the H1/H2 statistic is shifted towards 'burst' by T_bias = +0.114 (default, hog_gains), +0.064 (default, seq_novel), +0.107 (high_turnover, hog_gains), -0.036 (high_turnover, seq_novel) (one draw, 7 stems vs 9 U branches; the SE of a mean of 7 u-values is ~0.1, so none of these is distinguishable from 0).
* After adjusting for branch length and clade size, U branches carry fewer spurious gains than other internal branches (O/E ~0.5-0.6), stems about as many as predicted (O/E ~0.9-1.2): stem/U ratio of adjusted inflation = 1.78 (default, hog_gains), 2.05 (default, seq_novel), 2.22 (high_turnover, hog_gains), 1.73 (high_turnover, seq_novel). The direction (stems more inflated than controls) is the same in all 4 cases; the exact Poisson intervals of stems and U do not overlap in 3 of 4 (these intervals ignore overdispersion and the single simulation draw).
* Consequence for H1/H2: under the null, the inference itself can push stems above their controls. A burst claim should therefore (i) use the length/size-adjusted residual statistic (D6 NB-GLM) rather than raw counts, (ii) require an effect clearly larger than ~2x, and (iii) not interpret a 'burst' p-value near the threshold. A 'deficit' (fewer gains on stems) is not explained by this bias. More simulation replicates (seeds) would narrow these estimates (each costs ~12 min of Ancestra at 4 threads).
