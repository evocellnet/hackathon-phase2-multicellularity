W7 (EXPLORATORY): DO STEM DUPLICATIONS TOUCH ANCIENT LECA COMPLEXES? (Cox et al. CF-MS map x round-1 Ancestra HOGs)
=======================================================================================================
Scripts: scripts/r2/w7/w7_map.py, w7_enrich.py, w7_coretention.py (seed 11, nice). Tables in this folder.

CAVEATS (read first)
* Per-stem HOG duplication counts are method-dependent (EukaryOMA agreement <8%); a "duplication" here is an Ancestra call.
* The map contains only LECA-conserved complexes. It says nothing about animal-specific or plant-specific complexes.
* No CF-MS data exist for unicellular sisters of animals (or fungi, red algae, etc.). Nothing here is measured interaction
  data for the stems; we only ask whether duplicated genes belong to ancient complexes defined in other species.
* Complex clusters are walktrap clusters of predicted interactions, not curated complexes. Many are mixed; the labels are the
  commonest CORUM/ComplexPortal/GO name among members and are often wrong or "unnamed/mixed". Treat complex names loosely.
* Events are not independent (many duplications within one large family); Fisher p-values are optimistic.
* Mapping goes through UniProt accessions in only 13 of our species (Homo, Drosophila, C. elegans, Nematostella, Dictyostelium,
  yeast, Arabidopsis, Selaginella, Chlamydomonas, Phaeodactylum, Tetrahymena, Trypanosoma, P. falciparum). Strongylocentrotus,
  Ceratopteris and Euglena accessions do not match our IDs (0-0.5% mapped). Hence stem events can be tested only if the parent
  node is an ancestor of a mapped species. Rhodophyta: no mapped species, so strict test impossible (family-level only).

1. MAPPING RATES
* All 10,091 LECA OGs: 74.2% have an accession in our shared species; 72.6% map to >=1 of our proteins; 72.1% to >=1 protein in an
  Ancestra HOG. All 3,193 OGs of the complex map map (100%). Per-species: 90-100% of accessions map for 13 species
  (mapping_rates_by_species.tsv); 85-99% of mapped proteins sit in a non-singleton Ancestra HOG.
* An OG is linked to the HOG chain of each mapped protein (all ancestral genes up the tree). A duplication event (ancestral gene at
  the parent node splitting into >=2 copies on the branch) "involves a complex" if that ancestral gene is in the chain of a subunit.
* Complexes: cut_2014 (1,982 clusters; 1,979 of <=40 OGs kept; 125 with >=3 mapped subunits). Sensitivity: cut_1202 (122 complexes).
  Denominator = "testable" duplications (ancestral gene contains a gene of one of the 13 mapped species).

2. DUPLICATIONS ON STEMS VS CONTROLS (dup_fraction_by_group.tsv, dup_fraction_stem_vs_control_fisher.tsv)
Fraction of testable duplication events involving a LECA-complex subunit (stem; controls = 30 round-1 U branches: 63.6%; broad 50
internal controls: 61.3%):
  Metazoa 66.6% (821/1232)   Fisher vs U OR 1.14 p=0.034; vs broad OR 1.26 p=2e-4
  Fungal hyphae 72.1% (422/585)  OR 1.48 p<1e-4 (vs broad 1.64)
  Dictyostelia 61.2%  OR 0.90 p=0.23     Streptophyta 62.3%  OR 0.94 p=0.43
  PX clade 59.0%  OR 0.82 p=0.012        Volvocine 57.9%  OR 0.79 p=0.001
  Rhodophyta: not testable strictly (0 mapped species below parent node); family-level involvement 58% of all events.
Reading: animal and fungal stems are modestly enriched for duplications in ancient-complex genes; the PX and volvocine stems are
depleted; Dictyostelia and Streptophyta are at control level. No consistent multicellular signal. The effect on animals is small
(66.6% vs 63.6%) and not corrected for family age/size, so it may reflect that old, widely conserved families duplicate more.
Retained duplicates (both child copies present in >=2 species): the same pattern (Metazoa 68.1%, fungi 73.0%, controls 67.3%).

Per-complex enrichment (Fisher, BH; complex_enrichment_*.tsv):
* Pooled stems (excluding Rhodophyta) vs controls: 0 complexes at q<0.05 (cut_2014 and cut_1202).
* Metazoa stem vs controls: cut_2014 one complex at q<0.05, the "DNA replication factor C" cluster (16 events vs 0.33% control rate,
  OR 3.9, q=0.003; 14 of the 16 events trace to one subunit OG with no human gene name, so likely one expanded family, not RFC
  itself). cut_1202: "(ER)-localized multiprotein complex" cluster (GANAB/UGGT2/HYOU1 etc., 13 events, OR 4.9, q=0.002), mostly via
  one family. Nominal (uncorrected) hints: mitochondrial ATP synthase cluster (OR 4.5), nuclear pore cluster (OR 2.7), TREX, NuA4.
  Depleted on Metazoa stem: mitochondrial 55S ribosome. These are single-family, uncorrected-for-multiplicity leads, not findings.
* Animal-relevant complexes with duplicated subunits on the Metazoa stem (metazoa_stem_duplicated_complexes_*.tsv lists all with
  >=3 mapped subunits): Vacuolar ATPase (6 events, FER1L5/TCIRG1/ATP6V0C/ATP6V0D1), Nuclear pore (3), SNARE/syntaxin cluster
  (STX7, STX11, RAB2A; 4), Myosin V-related (MYO1G, CLTCL1), TIM22/23 carrier clusters (SLC25 carriers), mitochondrial RNase P/
  MMXD-type clusters. Counts are small; none is significant after BH.

3. CO-RETENTION ACROSS 187 SPECIES (coretention_by_complex_cut_2014.tsv, coretention_summary_cut_2014.txt)
Presence = species has >=1 gene in the OG's Ancestra family; subunits sharing identical families merged; 125 complexes with >=3
distinct subunits. Mean pairwise Jaccard J. Null: 1,000 permutations replacing each subunit with a random LECA OG of similar
prevalence (20 prevalence bins, seed 11).
* All species: median J 0.834 vs null 0.828 (median z 0.74); 30/125 complexes with p<0.05 (6 expected). Co-retention is only
  slightly above prevalence-matched random OGs, because LECA genes are nearly everywhere (high prevalence leaves little room).
* Multicellular (75 species): median J 0.854 (null 0.845), z 0.48, 13/125 p<0.05. Unicellular (90): J 0.796 (null 0.791), z 0.79,
  30/125 p<0.05. Paired difference in z (mc - uni): median -0.28, p=0.008 (53/125 complexes higher in multicellular).
  So against the null, complexes are if anything slightly LESS co-retained in multicellular lineages; raw J is higher in
  multicellular lineages (also when unicellular is subsampled to 75 species) but that reflects higher gene presence in sampled
  multicellular species, which the null absorbs. Weakest co-retention in multicellular: MCM, mito ribosome, ESCRT-II, complex I,
  nuclear pore (large dz, but loss of whole subunits in a few lineages, e.g. highly reduced fungi/algae, drives this); stronger:
  SF3A/spliceosome, E2F-RB, Pol III.
* Not phylogenetically corrected (species are not independent; groups differ in clade composition, quality, transcriptomes).

BOTTOM LINE
No strong or consistent preference of multicellular-stem duplications for ancient LECA-complex subunits. Animal and fungal stems
show small enrichment (OR 1.1-1.6), PX and volvocine stems depletion, others null; no complex survives pooled correction, and the one
Metazoa-stem hit is a single-family signal. Complexes remain co-retained about equally (slightly less above null) in multicellular
and unicellular lineages. Exploratory; subject to the method-dependence and coverage caveats above.
