ROUND 2 - W4 FOLLOW-UP: WHICH CELL-DEATH FAMILIES AND DOMAIN ARCHITECTURES CARRY THE MULTICELLULAR EXCESS
Produced 2026-10-30. DESCRIPTIVE AND EXPLORATORY ONLY. Nothing here changes the confirmatory R2-P3 result
(results/r2/w4/summary.txt; verification results/r2/verification/w4/VERIFICATION.txt).
Scripts: scripts/r2/w4_celldeath/01_extract.py, 02_tables.py, 03_panels.py, 04_figure.py (run with
conda_envs/ancestra/bin/python, nice, <= 8 processes). Figure: report/figures_v2/fig4b_celldeath_architectures.{png,pdf}.

WHAT WAS DONE
- Every protein carrying >= 1 of the 22 cell-death Pfam families (data/r2_sets/cell_death.tsv) was taken from the
  187 round-1 eukaryotic Pfam tables (Pfam-A 38.2, gathering cutoffs): 9,031 proteins. Per-species family counts
  reproduce the W4 count matrix exactly (11,105 protein-family pairs, 0 mismatches).
- Architecture = Pfam domains in sequence order. For readability, repeat families are merged into one token (all LRR_*
  -> LRR; TPR_* -> TPR; WD40-like propellers -> WD40; Ank_* -> ANK) and the winged-helix sub-domains that belong to
  the NB-ARC/NACHT module are dropped. NB-ARC and NACHT proteins (the "STAND" ATPases; 2,600 proteins) were classed by
  their C-terminal repeat (LRR/WD40/TPR/ANK = "NLR-like") and N-terminal effector domain (TIR, coiled-coil Rx_N, HET,
  HeLo, CARD/Death/DED/PYD, etc.).
- Lineages = the 10 eukaryotic comparisons of the W4 Pfam-wide scans (7 primary origins + 3 nested simple-to-complex
  events), each multicellular (MC) clade vs its sister clade (data/transitions.tsv). All data types are used, as in the
  scans; several sisters are transcriptomes (6 of 8 animal sisters, both dictyostelid sisters) or reversals (yeasts as
  the Pezizomycotina sister; Zygnematophyceae as the land-plant sister).
- Dense panels: for each simple-to-complex branch (plus the unicellular-to-complex origin of animals), the gene families
  that arose (sequence-novel) or duplicated on the branch and carry a cell-death domain, compared with the panel's
  control branches (one-sided Fisher test, BH over 32 event x kind rows; exploratory).
Caveat on names: Pfam "Peptidase_C14" (labelled caspase in the frozen set) also matches metacaspases. Outside animals
492 of 633 Peptidase_C14 proteins also carry the metacaspase domain (PF31181); in animals only 5 of 351 do. In the
figure "Caspase" = Peptidase_C14 without the metacaspase domain; "Metacaspase" = PF31181.

1. OVERALL SIZE OF THE EXCESS (148 primary proteomes)
- Mean cell-death proteins per species: complex multicellular 138 (median 56; 38 species), simple multicellular 18
  (median 15; 30), unicellular or coded 0 22 (median 12.5; 80). Per 10,000 proteins: 66 vs 14 vs 15.
  The excess is almost entirely a complex-multicellularity phenomenon, matching the three-state PGLS in W4.

2. WHICH FAMILIES DRIVE THE EXCESS IN WHICH LINEAGE (lineage_drivers.tsv; mean proteins per species, MC vs sister)
- Animals (266 vs 40 cell-death proteins per species; 119 vs 22 per 10k proteins). Biggest contributors to the MC-sister
  difference: Death domain (+91; 33% of the positive difference), NACHT (+52; 19%), CARD (+33; 12%), TIR (+25; 9%),
  caspase (+21; 8%). Death, CARD, DED, Bcl-2 and BH4 are absent from all 8 choanoflagellate sisters. Metacaspase is the
  only family lower in animals (0.5 vs 1.9). The counts are very uneven across animals: Death domains 287 in the sponge
  Amphimedon, 216 in Ephydatia and 211 in amphioxus, but 5-7 in C. elegans and Drosophila; NACHT 239 in the sea urchin
  and 134 in Hydra; NB-ARC 111 in Hydra. These are the known lineage-specific innate-immunity expansions of sponges,
  cnidarians, sea urchin and amphioxus [needs check].
- Hyphal fungi (22 vs 11): HET (+6.9; 49%), NACHT (+4.5; 32%), NB-ARC, HeLo. Caspase-like/metacaspase proteins are
  slightly LOWER (-2.3 each; chytrid sisters have ~6 metacaspases).
- Pezizomycotina (59 vs 4; sister = budding yeasts): HET alone +37 (64%; Botrytis 76, Neurospora 65), then NACHT +6.6,
  NB-ARC +4.4, HeLo +4.4. Agaricomycetes (32 vs 6): NACHT +13 (39%), metacaspase +8 (24%; Peptidase_C14 counts are the
  same proteins), NB-ARC +3.
- Dictyostelids: no excess (20 vs 33 per species). Only MACPF pore-formers are higher (12 vs 6); NACHT and TIR are lower.
- Streptophytes (79 vs 28): NB-ARC +42 (51%), TIR +18 (22%). Land plants (136 vs 10; Zygnematophyceae sister has none
  of NB-ARC): NB-ARC +84 (42%), TIR +46 (23%), TIR_2 +40 (20%; mostly the same TIR proteins), MACPF +7, metacaspase +5.
- Red algae (43 vs 9): NB-ARC +29 (59%), TIR +7, TIR_2 +7, metacaspase +3.
- Brown algae and relatives (PX filaments; 23 vs 20 per species, LOWER per 10k proteins: 12 vs 14): NB-ARC +8, mostly
  in the brown algae (Fucus 26, Dictyota 21, Ectocarpus 21); TIR is lower.
- Volvocines (18 vs 19): no excess in any family.
So the excess is carried by NB-ARC/TIR in plants and red algae, by HET/NACHT in filamentous fungi, and by death-fold
adaptors, NACHT and caspases in animals. The "metacaspases in algae" pattern was not found: metacaspase differences
are +1.8 to +3.3 per species in algae and plants and negative in hyphal fungi, volvocines and animals.

3. DOMAIN ARCHITECTURES (architectures_top.tsv, architectures_top_global.tsv, stand_classes.tsv, stand_excess.tsv)
NB-ARC/NACHT proteins account for 81-86% of the cell-death excess in streptophytes and red algae, 62-67% in
Agaricomycetes and land plants, 51% in hyphal fungi, 28% in animals and 19% in Pezizomycotina (where the excess is
mostly stand-alone HET proteins). The dominant architecture differs by lineage:
- Land plants: plant NLRs. NB-ARC-LRR (77 proteins in 6 species), TIR-NB-ARC-LRR (TNL; 38), Rx_N(coiled coil)-NB-ARC-
  LRR (CNL; 32), plus many NB-ARC-only and TIR-NB-ARC fragments or truncated NLRs. Per species: 41 with LRR, 18 with an
  N-terminal effector but no repeat, 25 with neither. Stand-alone TIR-TIR_2 proteins (TIR-only immune proteins) are 63.
  Streptophyte sisters (Mesostigma/Chlorokybus) have a few TIR-NB-ARC-TPR proteins, none with LRR.
- Red algae: NB-ARC-WD40 (73 proteins, all 5 MC species) and TIR-NB-ARC-WD40 (17, one species); none with LRR.
- Brown algae and relatives: NB-ARC-TPR (50 proteins, 6 species).
- Fungi: NPHP3_N-NACHT with or without WD40/ANK/TPR repeats (fungal NWD/NLR-like STAND proteins), Goodbye-NACHT, and in
  Pezizomycotina also PNP_UDP-NB-ARC-TPR. HET is mostly stand-alone (HET 144 of 185 in Pezizomycotina; HET-Het-6_barrel
  21) - HET-NACHT fusions are rare in these 5 genomes. HeLo occurs alone or in HeLo-NPHP3_N-NACHT.
- Animals: NACHT alone (425 proteins in 10 species), Death-NACHT (115), CARD-NACHT (53), NACHT-LRR (46),
  Death-NACHT-LRR (28); NB-ARC-TPR (91; Hydra and other non-bilaterians); one APAF1-like CARD-NB-ARC-WD40 in sponges.
  Caspases: Peptidase_C14 alone (214), CARD-caspase (32, 10 species), DED-caspase (15, 7 species). BIR: BIR alone (36)
  and BIR-RING (32; IAP-type). Bcl-2 mostly alone (99). Death domains are mostly alone (647) or with NACHT, ANK
  (ankyrin-Death, 34) and other partners.
- "Is the expansion one architecture type?" Within NB-ARC/NACHT: repeat-containing NLR-like proteins are the largest
  single class of the STAND excess in land plants (41 of 84 per species), streptophytes (18 of 42), red algae (20 of
  29), PX (5 of 8), Pezizomycotina (6 of 11) and hyphal fungi (3 of 5); in animals and Agaricomycetes the STAND excess
  is spread across NLR-like, effector-only and bare NACHT proteins (animals 16 / 16 / 33 per species). Bare NB-ARC or
  NACHT proteins are often fragments or mis-predicted gene models, so the split is approximate.
Summed over lineages, the largest excess is in architectures typical of immune / non-self receptors (NLR-like STAND
proteins, TIR proteins, HET), not in the core apoptotic executioners (caspase, Bcl-2, BIR), which are confined to animals.

4. DENSE PANELS: SIMPLE-TO-COMPLEX BRANCHES (panels_event_celldeath.tsv, panels_celldeath_families.tsv)
Cell-death families on event branches vs the panel's control branches (fraction of Pfam-annotated families):
- Animal origin (R1, unicellular -> complex): 18 of 474 annotated newly-arisen families carry a cell-death domain vs
  0.9% on control branches (odds ratio 4.4, q = 0.002); duplicated 20 of 1,139 vs 0.6% (OR 2.9, q = 0.003). New families
  include Bcl-2 (2 families), CARD, DED/Death-caspase, Death-SEFIR (IL-17 receptor-like), RHIM-TIR_2 (TRIF-like); duplicated
  ones include NACHT (269 genes), Death-NACHT-Roc-LRR (130), BIR-RING, TIR-LRR (Toll-like, 71) and MACPF.
- Florideophyceae (R5): 8 duplicated cell-death families vs 1.6 expected (q = 0.018), 5 of them NB-ARC-WD40 (two with
  TIR_2), plus metacaspase and AIF.
- Land plants (R3): 6 duplicated (2.9 expected; q = 0.54), including one NB-ARC-coiled coil-LRR family of 377 genes (the
  plant NLR expansion), metacaspases (incl. LSD1-metacaspase) and VPE. Chara (tip, transcriptome): 7 duplicated
  (q = 0.08), mostly NB-ARC-LRR (922 and 654 genes).
- Fucophycidae (R6, confirmatory P1 event): 10 duplicated (6.3 expected; q = 0.72): four NB-ARC-TPR families, two
  metacaspase, AIF, Bax inhibitor-1, VPE, SH2-NACHT. Dictyota: 4 (q = 0.50).
- Heterocystous cyanobacteria (R8): 16 duplicated (13.3 expected; q = 1): NACHT/NB-ARC-pentapeptide/WD40/CHAT, caspase-
  like and metacaspase families, MazF.
- Fungi (R2): Pezizomycotina 3 duplicated (metacaspase x2, HET) and 1 new (bacterial-type gasdermin), not enriched;
  Agaricomycetes, Neolecta and Tremella none. Endogone (tip) 8 single-gene Peptidase_C14 families (q = 0.008); single-gene
  tip families are unreliable (panel caveat D-P1/D-P6).
- Volvocines (R4): none on Astrephomene, V. carteri or V. africanus; Pleodorina 4 duplicated (q = 0.017), but these are
  ARM/HEAT-repeat proteins with weak HET/Peptidase_C14 matches and NPHP3_N-NACHT-WD40 - doubtful as cell-death genes.
- No new (sequence-novel) cell-death family arose on any internal simple-to-complex branch except one Pezizomycotina
  gasdermin family and 1 HET-domain family on the land-plant branch. Where cell-death families change at these events
  they are duplications of existing families, and in plants, red and brown algae these are NB-ARC families.

FUNCTION (plain language, with literature)
- NB-ARC and NACHT proteins are STAND ATPases. With C-terminal LRR (plants, animal NLRs), WD40, TPR or ANK repeats they
  are intracellular immune / non-self receptors: plant NLRs (TNLs and CNLs) detect pathogen effectors and trigger the
  hypersensitive response, a localised cell death [Jones & Dangl 2006, needs check; Lam 2004, needs check]; animal NLRs
  and APAF1 assemble inflammasomes and apoptosomes [needs check]. Fungal NLR-like proteins (NWD, HET-NACHT, HeLo-NACHT)
  mediate heterokaryon incompatibility, a programmed death of fused cells between genetically different individuals,
  and are proposed to act in non-self and pathogen recognition [Dyrka et al. 2014, needs check; Uehling et al. 2017,
  needs check; Paoletti & Saupe 2009, needs check]. HET domains are the toxic effectors of incompatibility; HeLo domains
  form membrane pores (Daskalov et al. 2016, needs check). TIR domains in plants and animals are immune signalling
  domains and some are NADases that execute cell death [Horsefield et al. 2019, needs check]. HET-domain hits outside
  fungi (land plants, volvocines, PX) should not be read as incompatibility genes.
- Death, CARD and DED are death-fold adaptors that assemble caspase-activating complexes in apoptosis and inflammation,
  but in animals they are also widely used in innate-immune signalling (e.g. MyD88, Toll/IL-1 receptor pathways)
  [Koonin & Aravind 2002, needs check; Aravind & Koonin 2002 "Classification of the caspase-hemoglobinase fold",
  needs check]. Caspases, Bcl-2 and IAP (BIR-RING) proteins form the core animal apoptosis machinery. Metacaspases are
  arginine/lysine-specific proteases that regulate cell death in plants, fungi and protists [Minina et al. 2020, needs
  check]; legumains/VPEs execute vacuolar cell death in plants [Hatsugai et al. 2004, needs check]; MACPF and gasdermins
  are pore-forming effectors (plant MACPF/CAD1 regulates immune cell death).
- The apoptosis machinery is ancestral to eukaryotes (Klim et al. 2018), and programmed cell death has been argued to
  be linked to the origin of multicellularity and to complexity (Durand et al. 2016) and to virus-host arms races
  (Iranzo et al. 2014). (These three are in LITERATURE.md.)

HONEST INTERPRETATION
- The confirmatory P3 excess is mostly an excess of immune / self-non-self recognition receptors (NB-ARC/NACHT NLR-like
  proteins, TIR proteins, HET), together with an animal-specific expansion of death-fold adaptors and caspases. Most of
  these proteins can trigger cell death, but in plants, red and brown algae and fungi they are best known as receptors
  that kill infected or incompatible cells, not as developmental programmed-cell-death genes (e.g. xylem or
  reproductive cell death, which in plants mostly uses other proteases).
- Bigger, longer-lived multicellular bodies are thought to need more immune surveillance, and fungal colonies that fuse
  need allorecognition. Both readings ("more cell-death machinery because multicellularity needs to kill cells" and
  "more immune receptors because multicellular organisms are larger, longer-lived targets that fuse with non-self") are
  compatible with these counts; the data cannot separate them. Removing NB-ARC + NACHT leaves the P3 PGLS positive
  (W4: p = 0.018), carried mainly by animal death-fold/caspase families and TIR, which are also partly immune.
- The pattern is lineage-specific, not a common recipe: plants and red algae expand NLR-like NB-ARC proteins with
  different repeat types (LRR vs WD40), brown algae NB-ARC-TPR, fungi HET and NACHT-WD40/ANK, animals death-fold and
  NACHT. Dictyostelids and volvocines show no excess.
- NLR genes are among the most rapidly birth-death-evolving families and are hard to annotate (fragments, pseudogenes,
  split gene models). Copy numbers vary 10-fold within lineages (Arabidopsis 164 NB-ARC vs Selaginella 20; sea urchin 239
  NACHT vs fly 1), so lineage means are driven by a few genomes.

CAVEATS
1. Descriptive. No new test of the multicellular effect; dense-panel Fisher tests are exploratory and treat families as
   independent.
2. Sister clades are small (2-8 species), partly transcriptomes or reversals; the animal and dictyostelid comparisons
   use transcriptome sisters, which under-count multidomain proteins.
3. Domain hits are Pfam GA only; architecture fragments (NB-ARC only) are frequent. Pfam detection is better in
   animals, plants and fungi.
4. Dense-panel families are annotated from up to 3 representatives (union of domains), so a family can carry a
   cell-death domain that is not shared by all members, and large NLR families are counted once regardless of size.
