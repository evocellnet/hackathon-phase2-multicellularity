RECURRENCE LADDER: WHICH RESULTS ARE SIGNIFICANT IN 1, 2, 3 ... INDEPENDENT EVENTS (D17; EXPLORATORY, POST HOC)
Written 2026-10-30 (D17, user-directed, after publication). Rules were fixed before computing: RULES.txt (12:53:40 CET).
Scripts: scripts/r2/recurrence_ladder/01-05. Inputs: existing per-event outputs only; no new per-event test, no new
reconstruction or search. Threads <= 2, nice 10. Nothing here changes any pre-registered result.

THE LEAD'S QUESTION, ANSWERED IN SHORT
"Did asking for a shared signature across all origins hide signals present in a subset of them?"
Mostly no. Counting events (>= 1, >= 2, >= 3 ...) instead of averaging does not reveal a new hypothesis-level result.
For no hypothesis is the number of significant independent events above chance after correcting across hypotheses.
At the family level, the ladder confirms the one recurrence result already in the report (contractions recur, expansions
do not), and it brings out one small, coherent addition: the cell-death receptor family NB-ARC is expanded at three
independent origins (animals, red algae, land plants), which is unlikely by chance for a pre-specified 21-family set.
Three things limit the exercise:
 (1) Most per-event tests cannot reach p < 0.05. Each event is compared with 5-20 control branches, so the smallest
     possible p is 1/(controls+1). Round-1 rank tests (20 controls) can reach 0.048; the rate test (10 controls) and every
     focused-panel test (5-9 controls) cannot. For those we also count events that rank above ALL their controls
     ("top rank"; chance probability 1/(controls+1)). Rows marked * in Figure 8a use this criterion.
 (2) Seven origins give few tiers. Under the null, about 0.33 of 7 origins are expected to be significant at 0.05, so 2 of 7
     already has p = 0.041 and 3 of 7 has p = 0.003. But with ~20 hypotheses per ladder, a single 2-of-7 does not survive
     correction.
 (3) The events are not fully independent. They share control branches, the species tree and the annotation pipeline, and
     nested events sit inside their origins. None of this is modelled.

DEFINITIONS (full text: RULES.txt)
- Independent units: the 7 primary round-1 eukaryotic origins (animals, fungal hyphae, dictyostelids, streptophytes,
  red algae, brown-algal (PX) filaments, volvocines). Nested simple->complex steps (Pezizomycotina, Agaricomycetes, land
  plants) have their own ladder, plus an "origin-collapsed" ladder: an origin counts once if it OR a nested step inside it
  is significant in the same direction. Focused panels (round 2) have separate ladders that are never merged with round 1,
  because they re-analyse the same origins: origins (internal branches), nested steps (internal), single-species branches
  (only where the controls are also single-species branches), cyanobacteria, and reversals.
- Per-event significance: the existing one-sided per-event p-value in the original direction. For rank tests,
  p = 1 - u + 0.5/(k+1). Nominal parametric p-values (Fisher/Wilcoxon) are labelled "nominal": they are not calibrated
  against control branches.
- Chance: number of significant events ~ Poisson-binomial(pi_e), with pi_e the exact per-event null probability
  (floor(alpha(k+1))/(k+1) for rank tests; alpha for nominal tests). Holm and BH are applied per tier, across the
  hypotheses of one ladder.
- Family scan (W4): Pfam families, transposon-masked, BH q < 0.1 within an event, in the multicellular clade vs its
  sister clade. Null: within each event, its significant families are reassigned among the families it tested (family
  shuffle). B = 10,000, seed 11; this reproduces the published B = 1,000 values. Per-family recurrence p: exact
  Poisson-binomial, BH across all 12,813 families. Post hoc sensitivities (added after seeing the first family results,
  stated here): a size-matched null that shuffles within deciles of family copy number per event (large families have more
  power in every event); leave-one-origin-out; and origins with genome-based sister clades only.

3. WHAT THE >= k VIEW ADDS TO THE ALL-ORIGINS AVERAGE
 - It adds nothing at the hypothesis level. The averages were null because most origins are individually null, not
   because a strong signal in two or three origins was diluted. For the core gene-content hypotheses (novelty, TF,
   adhesion/signalling, flagellar loss, co-option, rate), 0 of 7 origins is significant even singly.
 - It sharpens the cell-death result. The confirmatory PGLS excess (REPORT 7.2) is carried by lineage-specific receptors.
   In the family scan, NB-ARC is significantly expanded at three independent origins, TIR and NACHT at two. This is the
   "convergence on a function with different architectures" reading of REPORT 7.3, now shown to recur beyond chance for
   a pre-specified set. It depends on counting land plants (a nested step) for the streptophyte origin, and it is
   marginal under the size-matched null after correction (Holm 0.09).
 - It makes clear that recurrence across origins is mostly a contraction phenomenon. A substantial part of it is
   plausibly a sister-transcriptome artefact; a small, genome-sister-robust core remains (PDEase_I, k=2 excess p 0.01).
 - Recurrent in >= 3 independent origins and above chance after the stricter checks: no expanded family.
   Contracted: PDEase_I (4 origins, genome sisters, q 0.07) and Vint (4 origins, q 0.07, partly transcriptome sisters).
   Recurrent in >= 2: NB-ARC (expanded, 2 origins + land plants), TIR, NACHT (with nested steps), and 157 contracted
   families (list in recurrent_families_k2.tsv).

4. RECOMMENDATION FOR THE REPORT
 Worth adding as a short exploratory paragraph and Figure 8, framed as "a conjunction view does not rescue the nulls".
 Two concrete sentences: (i) NB-ARC expanded at three independent origins (animals, red algae, land plants) and TIR/NACHT
 at two, which supports the cell-death section; (ii) contractions recur, expansions do not, and part of the contraction
 recurrence is explained by transcriptome-based sister clades. Do not present any tier as a confirmatory finding.

CAVEATS
 Post hoc and exploratory, requested after all results were public. Per-event tests are coarse (few controls), so the
 ladders have little power and many rows cannot be significant by construction. Events share controls, tree and
 annotation; nested events and single-species branches are handled by separate ladders, not modelled. Annotation and
 transcriptome artefacts (bacterial-type glycosidases, inteins, contaminant families in sister transcriptomes) affect
 contractions. Nominal per-event tests (Fisher, within-stem Wilcoxon) are not calibrated and are shown to be inflated
 where control branches exist. Several hypotheses have correlated versions (3 architecture statistics, 4 co-option
 versions), which makes Holm conservative.
