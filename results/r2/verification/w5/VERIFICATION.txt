OVERALL VERDICT
  Arithmetic reproduces exactly. The headline "events keep more structurally new families" is NOT robust:
  it disappears after adjusting for family length/disorder plus branch depth (OR 1.18 [0.93, 1.49]), and it
  weakens to OR 1.23 / permutation p = 0.07 when the Foldseek search is made more sensitive (exhaustive),
  because the default k-mer prefilter misses more true homologs for event families than for control families.
  The simple->complex (nested) excess survives most variants (OR 1.5-2.3, p 0.001-0.03) but not branch-level
  regression with all covariates (OR 1.15-1.23, n.s.), and with 7 events it is fragile. Recommended wording:
  "exploratory; a possible excess of structurally unmatched families at simple->complex events, sensitive to
  search sensitivity and confounded with protein length/disorder; no general event effect".

ITEM 1 - Recompute fractions, pooled MH OR and branch permutation                                 PASS
  Events (19 internal origin/origin_complex/nested branches) 1226/2805 = 43.71 %, controls (55) 2054/6162 =
  33.33 %; MH OR 1.439 [1.310, 1.582] (Robins-Breslow-Greenland CI); identical to the producer.
  Per-panel ORs identical (R1 1.18, R2 1.09, R3 1.77, R4 2.95, R5 0.61, R6 2.60, R7 1.52, R8 1.22).
  Branch-label permutation within panel (20,000x): the producer's statistic (equal-weight branch mean difference,
  averaged over panels) gives one-sided p = 0.0037 (reproduces 0.004). With a family-weighted statistic (MH log
  OR as the permutation statistic) p = 0.015. Nested events: p = 0.005; origins: p = 0.29.
  Caveat: family-level CIs (MH, Fisher) treat families as independent and are far too narrow; families are
  clustered in 74 branches (GLMM unadjusted OR 1.50 [1.13, 2.00] has a 3x wider log-CI than MH).

ITEM 2 - Confounders                                                                              FAIL (effect not robust)
  Strong family-level predictors of "structurally new" (full GLMM, standardised): log mean length
  beta = -1.78 (z = -41), disorder fraction +0.81 (z = 21), low-complexity +0.13, family size -0.09, log outside
  target proteins -0.13 (p = 0.03); depth +0.09 (p = 0.11); transcriptome fraction n.s.
  Events differ from controls on exactly these: deeper (SMD 0.93), more disordered (SMD 0.49), shorter
  (SMD -0.27), larger families (0.36), more low-complexity (0.24) (branch-level means).
  Event effect (logistic GLMM, random intercepts panel + branch, n = 8,967 families, 74 branches):
    unadjusted                                  OR 1.50 [1.13, 2.00] p = 0.005
    + log outside proteins (producer's model)   OR 1.51 [1.13, 2.02]  (reproduces the producer's 1.45)
    + branch covariates (depth, search space, transcriptome fraction)   OR 1.22 [0.89, 1.67] p = 0.21
    + family covariates (length, disorder, LC, size)                    OR 1.27 [1.02, 1.58] p = 0.03
    full (all covariates)                       OR 1.18 [0.93, 1.49] p = 0.16
    full, by kind: nested OR 1.73 [1.20, 2.51] p = 0.003; origin OR 1.04 [0.82, 1.33]
  Branch-level quasibinomial regression (74 branches, panel fixed effects): unadjusted OR 1.45 [1.13, 1.85];
    adjusted OR 0.98 [0.84, 1.15]; nested 1.23 [0.95, 1.58], origin 0.94.
  Covariate-adjusted branch permutation (residuals from a no-event logit with all covariates): all events
    p = 0.34; nested p = 0.07; origins p = 0.81. With family covariates only: all p = 0.07, nested p = 0.006.
  Interpretation: the search-space adjustment the producer reported does not touch the main confounders.
  Depth is partly collinear with "nested" (nested events sit deep by construction), so adjusting for depth
  may over-adjust for nested events; length/disorder are not collinear and they alone remove most of the
  overall effect.

ITEM 3 - Sampling cap (<= 200 families per branch, seed 11)                                       PASS
  For all 82 sampled event/control branches the available counts match the ladder (classified.parquet,
  seq_novel); every sampled family is a ladder seq-novel family. 33 branches were capped (12 event, 21 control).
  On capped branches, the mean ID-rank of sampled families is 0.498 (range 0.47-0.53; 0.5 expected for a random
  draw), i.e. not the first 200 by ID; sampled vs unsampled family sizes are equal (8.8 vs 8.8 genes). The cap

ITEM 4 - Heterogeneity, per panel, leave-one-panel-out                                            PASS (as descriptive) / heterogeneity is large
  Per-panel ORs 0.61-2.95; R5 goes the other way (0.61 [0.45, 0.81]). Leave-one-panel-out MH OR stays
  1.29-1.61 (lowest without R6, highest without R5), so no single panel drives the pooled family-level OR.
  But per panel there are only 2-4 event branches; no single-panel branch permutation is < 0.05 (producer:
  0.07-0.76). The family-level LOO is therefore stable only in the same over-precise sense as the MH CI.

ITEM 5 - Is "structurally new" mostly "no fold" (short / disordered / low-complexity)?            FAIL (largely yes)
  metapredict 3.1.01 for all 81,572 query representatives (correlation with the project's precomputed disorder
  files where both exist: r = 0.9997). Among event+control families:
    median mean length: new 211 aa vs old 519 aa; families < 100 aa are 92 % "new" (13.7 % of all new
    vs 0.7 % of old); median disorder fraction new 0.49 vs old 0.33; 48.6 % of new families are >= 50 %
    disordered vs 32 % of old. Low-complexity differs little (0.125 vs 0.118 median).
  Events carry more of these hard-to-match families (short < 100 aa: 8.1 % vs 4.2 %; >= 50 % disordered:
  41 % vs 37 %). A large part of "structurally new" is "too short or too disordered to give a significant,
  >= 50 %-coverage 3Di+AA hit", not a demonstrated new fold.
  In a "foldable" subset (mean length >= 150 aa and disorder < 0.5; n = 4,698): event 25.7 % vs control 20.4 %
  new; GLMM unadjusted OR 1.49 [1.09, 2.03], full OR 1.33 [0.99, 1.78] p = 0.055; nested 2.25 [1.41, 3.61],
  origin 1.13. So a residual nested-event signal remains among foldable families.

ITEM 6 (added) - Search sensitivity: exhaustive re-search of all 3,280 producer-"new" families        FAIL (differential)
  All "new" families on event/control branches (8,287 queries) re-searched with foldseek --exhaustive-search 1
  (no k-mer prefilter), 3Di+AA, E < 1e-3, against the whole panel DB, outside-clade filter and >= 50 % query
  cover applied by me. Negative control: R5 queries with AA+3Di shuffled by one shared permutation, exhaustive
  mode: 0 of 908 queries with any passing hit, so the extra hits are not composition artefacts.
  Exhaustive search finds an outside structural homolog for 21 % of the producer's "new" families, and more
  often on events (23.0 %) than controls (19.5 %): R1 32 vs 20 %, R2 39 vs 23 %, R4 36 vs 24 %, R5 36 vs 16 %
  (R3, R7 equal; R6 23 vs 31 %; R8 ~0).
  With the exhaustive definition: events 944/2805 = 33.7 % vs controls 1653/6162 = 26.8 %; MH OR 1.23
  [1.11, 1.36]; branch permutation one-sided p = 0.073; nested MH OR 1.48 [1.29, 1.70], permutation p = 0.030;
  origins 1.06, p = 0.46. GLMM: unadjusted OR 1.32 [0.97, 1.81]; full 1.21 [0.92, 1.60]; nested full
  1.79 [1.15, 2.78] p = 0.009; foldable subset nested 2.30 [1.40, 3.79]; branch-level adjusted nested 1.15 (n.s.).
  About a third of the overall effect is therefore an artefact of prefilter sensitivity.

WHAT THE RESULT CAN AND CANNOT MEAN
  Can: the numbers are computed correctly from the hit tables; about half of sequence-novel families have a
  structural match outside the clade among panel proteomes (more with exhaustive search); the default search
  slightly favours calling event families "new".
  Can (weakly, exploratory): sequence-novel families gained at the 7 simple->complex stems lack an outside
  structural match somewhat more often than control-branch families, even among longer, mostly ordered proteins
  and with exhaustive search (OR about 1.5-2.3 at family level; p 0.001-0.03 depending on the model), but not
  in branch-level regression with all covariates. With 7 events in 6 panels and heavy heterogeneity this is a
  lead, not a finding.
  Cannot: claim that event branches in general (or origins of simple multicellularity) gain more structurally
  new families - this does not survive adjustment (OR 1.18 [0.93, 1.49]) or a more sensitive search (p = 0.07).
  Cannot: call these "new folds". ProstT5 3Di is a sequence-predicted proxy, short and disordered proteins
  dominate the "new" class, the structural search space is only the panel (not the 187 round-1 proteomes
  used for sequence novelty), and no predicted structures or pLDDT were examined.
  The reported search-space-adjusted OR 1.45 [1.08, 1.93] should not be presented as "adjusted": it adjusts
  for the one covariate that barely matters.
