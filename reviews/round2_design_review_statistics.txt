Statistical review of DESIGN_ROUND2.md v0 (biostatistics / comparative methods), 2026-10-29

Verdict: the design is sound in outline, but the confirmatory family as written cannot reach a decision. Six changes, all within scope.

1. Family size and attainable p. Holm over five tests sets the first threshold at 0.01. An exact one-sided sign test over n units has a minimum p of 2^-n, so 6 units can never pass (1/64 = 0.016). P1 has about 6 independent units (R2's two fungal events share one fit), and P5 has 7-8. Fix one statistic, direction and unit for each test now. Combine per-event in-run permutation p-values (Stouffer, k >= 30 controls) and report sign counts only as descriptions. Move P5 to exploratory: W6 is already labelled exploratory, a ratio of two noisy divergences saturates on deep branches, and ProstT5 error grows with divergence. Move P4 too: TF expansion in animals and plants is known, and round-1 H4 and controls C05/C09 used these families. Confirmatory family: P1-P3, Holm over 3.

2. Winner's curse in P1. The three round-1 events were chosen because p = 0.016. Make the confirmatory P1 the unseen events (Volvox, Florideophyceae, complex brown algae). Use heterocystous cyanobacteria only in sensitivity: horizontal transfer and a eukaryotic reference set make "sequence-novel" non-comparable. Pool all events as a secondary estimate. Rough power, taking the per-event residual SD as about 1.0 log unit (from the round-1 control spread), at a one-sided alpha of 0.0167: 3 new events give 18% power at 2-fold and 41% at 3-fold; 6-7 units give about 35% at 2-fold and 75% at 3-fold. Expect an estimate, not a decision.

3. Meta-analysis. With k <= 7, use Hartung-Knapp with the floor q* = max(1, q) (Roever et al. 2015). Round 1's unmodified HKSJ CI for 3 events, [0.38, 0.87], was narrower than DerSimonian-Laird's [-0.80, 2.04]. Handle nesting with one effect per panel or with CR2 cluster-robust variance. Report tau^2 and the prediction interval.

4. In-run controls and depth. A per-panel NB fit with 4 covariates on 20-50 branches overfits. Fit one NB GLM on the controls of all panels (panel intercepts, shared slopes). Exclude branches inside the multicellular crown. Depth imbalance will persist, so pre-specify the sister-clade stem (same parent, same depth) as the matched comparison. Add distance to the nearest outgroup as a covariate, because homology-detection failure inflates "sequence-novel" counts as divergence grows (Weisman et al. 2020).

5. P2-P3 copy number. log(count/proteome) fails on zeros and assumes 1:1 scaling with proteome size. Primary: PGLS (phylolm, Pagel's lambda) of log(count + 0.5) with log proteome size as a covariate, on genome proteomes (transcriptomes and BUSCO < 50% in sensitivity). Code state as one one-sided contrast, multicellular vs unicellular; complex vs simple is secondary. Sensitivity: a phylogenetic Poisson-lognormal or NB GLMM (MCMCglmm/brms). With few origins, PGLS significance can rest on one clade (Uyeda et al. 2018), so require the sign to survive a leave-one-origin-out jackknife. Summed counts are dominated by large families (SH3, PDZ); use the equal-weight mean of standardised per-family log-counts. Choose PGLS or the sister contrasts, not both. Hash the external set lists; round-1 Pfam scans were seen.

6. Scans and effect sizes. Fit a per-family NB GLM of each clade vs its sister (log-proteome offset), with BH q < 0.1 within each event after transposon masking. Calibrate recurrence (significant in >= 2 events) by permuting event labels, not with a binomial null. Shrink fold-changes (ashr) before ranking. Report effect sizes first: per-event rate ratios with 95% CI, the pooled ratio with a modified-HK CI and prediction interval, excess family counts, per-state fold change with lambda; then p-values.
